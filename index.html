About

Research on the history of central banking and the political economy of money.

I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Sciences Po Paris.

I am particularly interested in how central banks acquire authority, how economic ideas move across policy arenas, and how struggles over inflation, growth, and financial governance reshape state institutions. I also write policy-oriented work on monetary policy, central bank independence, and European economic governance.

Research

Research interests

History of central banking

The political, institutional, and intellectual history of central banks in the twentieth century, with a focus on the Bank of Italy.

Political economy of monetary regimes

Inflation, stabilisation, exchange-rate arrangements, and the politics of monetary authority in advanced economies.

European monetary integration

The making of EMU, the role of economic ideas and expertise in European governance, and the contribution of domestic elites to monetary convergence.

Central bank activism and independence

Evolving mandates, developmental central banking, and the comparative politics of central bank autonomy across historical and contemporary contexts.

Journal article

How Credit Policy Ends: Central Bankers and the Rise and Fall of Credit Controls in Italy, 1973–1983

This article examines the decline of credit controls in Italy and its relationship to the making of European monetary integration. Drawing on archival sources from the Bank of Italy and the Historical Archive of the EU, it argues that the delegitimisation of credit controls was driven by domestic "policy learning" among reform-oriented economists — figures such as Tommaso Padoa-Schioppa, Mario Monti, and Paolo Baffi — rather than by external pressures from European harmonisation. The central bank acted as an "epistemic hub" that anticipated and then fed into broader European convergence processes, revealing the active agency of domestic elites in shaping the path of Italian monetary integration.

Journal article

A European Credit Council? Lessons from the History of Italian Central Banking after World War II

By formulating a proposal for the "democratisation" of the ECB through the establishment of a European Credit Council, Eric Monnet makes a fundamental contribution to the contemporary debate on central banking. This article argues that the intellectual optimism with which post-war institutional set-ups are often viewed can be misleading. Focusing on the Italian case, it shows how the attempt to democratically control the central bank was neither linear nor necessarily successful: the establishment of a credit council did not prevent the Bank of Italy from enjoying a high degree of de facto independence, while the broader system of credit policy often degenerated into irrational and opaque practices.

Policy brief

Trump vs. the Fed: una storia tra le storie

The re-election of Donald Trump has reignited an old and complex debate: the relationship between the President of the United States and the Federal Reserve. Analysing episodes from Nixon-Burns to Volcker, Reagan, and Greenspan, this policy brief argues that the independence of the Federal Reserve is more of a myth than a reality and that understanding the Fed requires attention to politics, individual personalities, and the institutional dynamics of the US system — not just the legal framework.

Policy brief

Il commercio internazionale tra protezionismo e liberalizzazione: la lezione di Guido Carli

In the wake of Trump's "Liberation Day" tariff announcements and the ensuing financial turmoil, this policy brief draws on the career of Guido Carli — Governor of the Bank of Italy (1960–1975) and a founding figure of the postwar international monetary order — to reflect on the tensions between protectionism and liberalisation. Carli's lesson, grounded in cooperation, Europeanism, and Atlanticism, offers a historically informed compass for navigating a new era of economic nationalism and strategic trade conflict.
