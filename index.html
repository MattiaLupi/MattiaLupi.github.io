Mattia Lupi

Researcher in Political Science and Economic History

I work at the intersection of political science, economic history, and the history of central banking.

My research focuses on the role of central banks as political actors, the evolution of monetary regimes, international economic institutions, the making of European monetary integration, and the changing impact of economic ideas and expertise.

About

Research on monetary institutions, political conflict, and economic governance.

This website gathers my research profile, selected conference participation, and current work in political science, economic history, and the history of central banking.

I am particularly interested in how institutions acquire authority, how economic ideas move across policy arenas, and how struggles over inflation, growth, and financial governance reshape the state.

Research

Research interests

History of central banking

The political, institutional, and intellectual history of central banks in the twentieth century.

Political economy of monetary regimes

Inflation, stabilization, exchange-rate arrangements, and the politics of monetary authority.

Italy and postwar capitalism

State capacity, social conflict, and institutional change in the Italian political economy after 1945.

Europe and international institutions

The making of EMU, European governance, and the role of international economic organizations.
